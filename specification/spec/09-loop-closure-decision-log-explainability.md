# Section 9 — Loop Closure, Decision Log, and Explainability

*Build-ready specification, Phase 2. This is the **closure** of the specification: the section that ties Sections 1–8 into one closed loop and makes that loop **auditable, queryable, and explainable end to end**. Where the earlier sections each own a stage — Section 2 the raw data, Section 3 the derived metrics, Section 4 the state estimate, Section 5 the plan, Section 6 the adaptation, Section 7 the recovery and taper, Section 8 the conversational surface — Section 9 owns the connective tissue that records what the loop did and lets anyone reconstruct **why**: the end-to-end trace from raw data to plan change, the **first-class, queryable decision-log schema** that formalizes the records Section 6 emits, the data contract the explanation surface (Section 8 §8.3) reads, the propagation of confidence so low confidence is shown and never hidden as false precision, and the surfacing of the Section 2 vendor sidecar-versus-own divergence checks. It conforms to `research/00` as the decision authority: explainability as a first-class constraint and the guarantee that every applied adaptation is logged with its inputs and the rule that fired (`research/00` §1.6), raw-over-derived (`research/00` §1.5), and load-bearing finding 8 (every determinant carries a confidence, low confidence widens the guardrails and is surfaced, not hidden — `research/00` Part 2 finding 8). It cites `research/05` §6 for the end-to-end pipeline and §5.6 for the logged-decision requirement, and `decisions/01` for the promotion of the decision log from an implied byproduct to a first-class queryable record. This section **formalizes machinery the rest of the spec already produces and consumes; it does not re-invent it**: Section 6 §6.1 already guarantees the emission of a record for every applied change, and Section 8 §8.3 already specifies what the explanation surface needs from that record — Section 9 defines the schema that sits between them. Per the project conventions, every rule is stated here in full; the evidence behind it lives in the cited docs and is not re-argued.*

## 9.1 What this section owns, and the boundaries it respects

Section 9 owns the **loop-closure and explainability layer**: the specification of the closed loop as one traceable circuit (§9.2), the decision-log schema and its queryability contract (§9.3), the data contract that the explanation surface reads (§9.4), the rule that confidence travels with every record and every athlete-facing quantity (§9.5), and the surfacing of the vendor sidecar-versus-own corroboration checks (§9.6). It is the discharge of the project's explainability requirement — that "every applied adaptation is logged with its inputs and the rule that fired, so the autonomous system can explain each change" (`research/00` §1.6; `research/05` §5.6) — and of `decisions/01`'s instruction to "formalize the §5.6 log as a queryable structure." Four boundaries fix what this section is and is not.

- **Section 9 formalizes the log; Section 6 produces the entries.** Section 6 is the producer: every arbitration outcome, gate decision, re-anchoring, weekly re-solve, and re-periodization emits a record of what changed, which signals drove it, and which arbitration rung resolved it (§6.1, §6.10). Section 9 does not decide anything and does not emit records itself; it defines the **schema** those records conform to, the **provenance links** that tie them to the data and state that produced them, and the **query interface** that makes them a first-class, queryable structure rather than an append-only text log (`decisions/01`). The division is exact: Section 6 guarantees that nothing changes the plan without emitting a record; Section 9 guarantees that every emitted record is complete, well-formed, provenance-linked, and queryable.
- **Section 9 supplies the explanation data; Section 8 renders the language.** The natural-language explanation surface — the parser, the plain-language answers, the confidence phrasing — is Section 8's (§8.3, §8.6). Section 9 owns only the **data contract underneath it**: the guarantee that the four already-computed sources Section 8 §8.3.1 reads (the decision-log record, the current state model, the derived metrics, the gap-to-goal decomposition) are addressable, complete, and internally consistent, so that the language model has faithful material to translate and never has to fabricate. Section 8 is the consumer; Section 9 is the contract that makes faithful consumption possible.
- **Section 9 records and surfaces; it does not compute metrics or state.** The derived metrics are Section 3's, the state estimate and its confidences are Section 4's, the plan is Section 5's, the adaptation decisions are Section 6's, the taper and recovery behavior are Section 7's. Section 9 reads all of them into a durable, queryable record and links them into a trace; it re-derives nothing. In particular it does not recompute a confidence, re-run an arbitration, or re-project a pace — it captures what those sections produced, with enough provenance that the computation could be **replayed and reproduced** from the record (§9.3.5).
- **Section 9 closes the loop; it opens no new decision path.** Nothing in this section changes the plan, the state, or the metrics. Like Section 8, it is a layer over the deterministic engine, not a part of it: it observes, records, links, and surfaces. The one thing it *surfaces that no earlier section acts on* — the vendor sidecar-versus-own divergence (§9.6) — is explicitly **corroboration only, never a decision input** (`research/00` §1.5; §2.3.6), preserving raw-over-derived at the presentation layer exactly as the engine preserves it at the decision layer. This surfacing entails the single trivial derivation Section 9 performs: a **presentation-layer comparison** of the system's own metric against the quarantined vendor counterpart to compute a divergence (§9.6). This is the one exception to "re-derives nothing" in the boundary above, and it is deliberately not a metric or state computation — it compares two numbers the prior sections already produced (Section 3/Section 4's own estimate and Section 2's ingested sidecar value) and never feeds the result back into any decision.

Everything Section 9 records and surfaces operates on the raw-derived metrics, the owned state model, the plan, and the deterministic decision log — never on the quarantined vendor sidecar as a driver, and only ever showing a sidecar value as clearly-labeled corroboration when the athlete asks or when a divergence is worth flagging (§9.6; `research/00` §1.5). Because the adaptation engine is fully deterministic (§6.1), the decision log is not a lossy summary of what happened — it is a **complete and replayable account** of it, which is the property that makes the whole system explainable rather than merely narratable.

## 9.2 The closed loop, end to end

The system is a closed loop: raw data becomes metrics, metrics become a state estimate, the state estimate and the goal become a plan, incoming data adapts the plan, and the adapted plan produces the next session, whose data re-enters at the top. `research/05` §6 states the cycle; Section 9's job is to specify it as **one traceable circuit** in which every stage names where the prior section plugs in and leaves a durable record, so that any prescription the athlete receives can be traced backward, step by step, to the raw streams that justified it.

### 9.2.1 The circuit and where each section plugs in

Read clockwise, the loop is the composition of the prior eight sections, each consuming the previous stage's output and producing the next stage's input:

1. **Raw ingest and quality-gating (Section 2).** Every activity is mapped onto the vendor-neutral canonical schema (record, RR, context streams) and stamped with per-session and per-stream quality verdicts; vendor-derived numbers are quarantined in the sidecar (§2.2–§2.4, §2.3.6). This is the loop's only entry point for device data — the raw substrate everything downstream computes on.
2. **Per-session features and derived metrics (Section 3).** The gated streams yield the grade-adjusted-pace substrate, the reconciled per-session load, the 42/7 fitness/fatigue/form chart, the training-response signals (decoupling, EF, HR-at-pace), the resting-HRV trend, the durability drift, and the advisory load-dynamics context — all computed only from the raw streams, never from the sidecar (§3.3–§3.10).
3. **State estimate (Section 4).** The metrics update the owned determinant profile — CS/D′, vVO₂max, functional threshold pace, the EF economy proxy, durability, the individual Riegel *b*, plus the HR anchors — each through the scalar-Kalman filter, each carrying a trend and a **confidence** (§4.2–§4.3). This is the loop's shared memory, the thing that makes the response cumulative rather than reactive (§4.1, §6.2).
4. **Race-pace projection and gap-to-goal (Section 1 / Section 4 §4.7).** The state estimate is translated into a projected average race pace under the goal course and expected conditions, and the projection is decomposed by determinant into the gap-to-goal — how much of the distance to the target pace is threshold versus VO₂max versus durability versus economy (§1.4; `research/05` §4).
5. **Plan generation (Section 5).** The state estimate, the goal contract, the athlete constraints, and the calendar are turned into a periodized plan of relative-anchored, machine-representable workouts targeting the largest addressable gaps (§5.1–§5.8).
6. **Adaptation across five timescales (Section 6).** The long-term, weekly, recent-workout, day-of readiness, and optional intra-workout loops read the metrics, the state, and the plan, and compose one prescription each day under the arbitration ladder, protected by the injury-risk verdict and the safety hard flags (§6.2–§6.8). This is where the plan is scaled, tweaked, re-anchored, or regenerated — and where every change emits a decision-log record (§6.1).
7. **Recovery and taper (Section 7).** The recovery-week slots and the taper Section 6 schedules are given their depth, shape, and individualization, driving the TSB the chart produces toward the race-day form band (§7.2–§7.7).
8. **Prescribed session → observed outcome → back to ingest.** The athlete executes the prescription; the resulting activity re-enters at stage 1, closing the loop.

The **conversational surface (Section 8)** sits across the loop rather than in it: it injects athlete free-text as structured inputs at the stage-6 entry points (§6.8.1) and reads the loop's records and state back out as plain-language explanation (§8.3). The loop turns without it; it makes the loop legible and negotiable (§8.2).

### 9.2.2 The traceability property

Section 9's contribution to this circuit is a single invariant it makes the rest of the system honor:

> **Every prescription the athlete receives is traceable, backward through the loop, to the raw data and the rules that produced it — with no undocumented step.**

Concretely, given any session the athlete was prescribed, the record must let a reader (or the explanation surface, or an auditor) reconstruct the full chain: which decision-log entry set today's session (stage 6), which state-model version that decision read (stage 3), which derived-metric snapshot and which prior sessions fed that state (stages 2 and 1), which plan the session belongs to and which generation produced it (stage 5), and which arbitration rung resolved the day (§6.5). This is not a new computation — it is the requirement that each stage **persist enough provenance** that the composition is reconstructible. The decision log (§9.3) carries the stage-6 record and its links up the chain; the state-model version history (§9.3.4) carries stage 3; the per-session feature provenance Section 3 already attaches (§3.4, "carried as a feature so … any audit can see how each day's load was derived") carries stage 2; and the plan version history (§9.3.4) carries stage 5. Section 9's schema is what binds these into one queryable trace, so that the deterministic loop is not merely *deterministic* but *demonstrably* so after the fact.

This invariant is the mechanism behind the project's explainability constraint (`research/00` §1.6) and its clearest competitive differentiator and safest freedom-to-operate posture (`research/06` §8, §11): the differentiator is the transparent, raw-data-derived engine whose every move is on the record, not the conversational veneer over it.

## 9.3 The decision-log schema

`research/00` §1.6 and `research/05` §5.6 require that **every applied adaptation is logged with its inputs and the rule that fired**; Section 6 §6.1 makes that emission a guarantee; `decisions/01` promotes the log "from an implied byproduct to a first-class, queryable explanation record." Section 9 defines that record. The schema is the enumerable core of this section and is stated in full below.

### 9.3.1 The record and its fields

A **decision-log record** is emitted by Section 6 for every applied change to the plan or the state, and for every safety verdict, whether or not it changed anything (a green readiness gate that let the planned session stand is still a decision on the record, because "why was today *not* changed?" is a question the athlete may ask). Each record is an append-only, immutable entry (§9.3.5) carrying the following fields:

| Field | Contents | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `record_id` | Unique, stable identifier for this decision. | Assigned on emission |
| `emitted_at` | Timestamp the decision was made (UTC), plus the athlete-local date. | Section 6 |
| `effective_from` / `effective_to` | The plan window the change applies to (today's session; the next 1–3 sessions; next week; the remaining mesocycle), so a query by date resolves which records governed any given day. | Section 6 |
| `decision_type` | Which write-path produced it, from the fixed set: `readiness_gate` (scale today), `recent_workout_tweak` (next 1–3 sessions), `re_anchor` (state update via Section 4's filter), `weekly_resolve` (short-term re-plan), `re_periodization` (full re-plan via Section 5), `injury_risk_verdict`, `taper_action`, `recovery_week`, `goal_change_applied`, `hard_flag`. | Section 6 §6.2–§6.4, §6.3; Section 7 |
| `loop` | Which of the five timescales owns the decision: `long_term` / `short_term` / `recent_workout` / `readiness` / `intra_workout` (or `safety` for a verdict/flag that sits above the loops). | Section 6 §6.2 |
| `trigger` | The specific trigger that fired, drawn from the triggering loop's defined trigger set (e.g. "run of ≥3 amber days", "qualifying maximal effort", "CTL ramp above cap", "athlete travel constraint", "mesocycle boundary", "bone-pattern pain reported"). | Section 6 §6.2 |
| `inputs` | The signals present at decision time, each as a `{name, value, source_ref, confidence, quality_flag}` tuple — the derived metrics read (CTL/ATL/TSB, HRV verdict, decoupling/EF, load-dynamics context), the state determinants and their confidences, the readiness channels, the gap-to-goal decomposition, the injury-risk context, and any athlete-supplied input (§8.4) that fed the change. `source_ref` links each input to its origin (the metric snapshot, the state-model version, the session, or the chat input). | Sections 3, 4, 6, 8 |
| `arbitration_rung` | Which rung of the §6.5.2 ladder resolved the decision — `safety` / `readiness` / `short_term_guardrail` / `recent_reanchor` / `long_term_ambition` — and, where a conflict was resolved, the losing signal(s), so the record shows not just what won but what it beat (the meta-rule application, §6.5.3). | Section 6 §6.5 |
| `guardrails_applied` | Which caps or asymmetries bound the outcome: ramp-rate cap, ~10%/week volume cap, monotony cap, mandatory recovery cadence, up-regulation damping on low confidence, HRV-unavailable widening. Records where the ambition was *held back*, not only where a signal pushed a change. | Section 6 §6.2.2, §6.7 |
| `change` | The resulting plan/state diff: the affected element (a session instance, a set of upcoming sessions, a week, a determinant, the whole plan) with its before → after values (e.g. "Tue key session: VO₂max 5×3′ → threshold 4×5′"; "threshold pace 4:05/km → 4:01/km"; "week 7 volume 68 km → 61 km"). A `re_anchor` records the determinant move; a `re_periodization` links to the new plan version (§9.3.4). | Sections 4, 5, 6, 7 |
| `confidence_context` | The confidence of the determinant(s) the decision leaned on, and whether low confidence damped the action (the §6.7 up-regulation asymmetry), so the record shows how certain the system was when it acted. | Section 4 §4.3.2; Section 6 §6.7 |
| `athlete_owned_flag` | Set when the record is a *proposal* awaiting the athlete (a goal-contract change proposal) or an athlete-facing *safety pathway* instruction, distinguishing the two matters the system does not own (the goal contract, §6.8; the athlete's clinical action and return-to-run clearance on the safety pathway the system issues, §6.4; `research/00` AUT-02, AUT-08) from the apply-and-notify majority. | Section 6 §6.4, §6.8 |
| `state_model_version` / `plan_version` | The version identifiers of the state model read and the plan written, linking the record into the version chain (§9.3.4). | §9.3.4 |

Every field is either a fact the deterministic engine already computed or a link to one; **no field requires a new inference**, which is what keeps the log a faithful account rather than a reconstruction. Numeric values in the record are the engine's own — the log never rounds away a confidence or a divergence to make a tidier story.

### 9.3.2 The two things every record must carry for the explanation surface

Section 8 §8.3.1 fixes what an explanation reads: for any change, "the inputs that were present, and the arbitration rung that fired." The schema is designed so those two are **always present and always addressable** for every record — they are the `inputs` and `arbitration_rung` fields, made mandatory precisely because they are the spine of every "why" answer (§8.3.1). Section 8 declared this its principal upstream dependency (§8.7); Section 9 discharges it by making a record ill-formed — rejected at emission — if it lacks either. A decision that fired without recording the signals it read or the rung that resolved it is, by construction, not explainable, and the schema does not permit it. This is the concrete meaning of "every applied adaptation is logged with its inputs and the rule that fired" (`research/00` §1.6): the log schema *enforces* the guarantee that Section 6 makes.

### 9.3.3 Queryability

`decisions/01` requires the log be **queryable**, not merely a chronological stream. The schema supports query along the axes the explanation surface's question types (Section 8 §8.3.1) demand:

- **By date / session** ("why is today easy?") → resolve every record whose `effective_from`/`effective_to` window covers the day, ordered by arbitration precedence, so the answer is the record(s) that actually governed that day.
- **By plan element** ("why did my long run move to Sunday?") → resolve the record whose `change` touched that element, returning the trigger (the scheduling constraint) and the weekly re-solve that moved it.
- **By change type / magnitude** ("why did you cut this week's volume?") → resolve `weekly_resolve` / `readiness_gate` records with a downward `change`, returning the guardrail or readiness signal that drove the cut.
- **By determinant** ("why do you think my threshold improved?") → resolve `re_anchor` records for that determinant, returning the qualifying efforts and corroboration that the Section 4 filter acted on.
- **By source** ("did my message about travelling change anything?") → resolve records whose `inputs` carry an athlete-supplied `source_ref`, closing the finding-7 loop (§9.5; Section 8 §8.6) that lets the athlete see their input change the plan.

The "am I on track?" question is answered not from the log but from the **current state model and gap-to-goal** (Section 8 §8.3.1, source 2 and 4); Section 9's contract is that those are addressable as a current snapshot alongside the historical log, so the explanation surface reads both through one interface.

### 9.3.4 Provenance and the version chain

A decision reads a state model and writes a plan; to make the trace of §9.2.2 reconstructible, both the state model and the plan are **versioned**, and each decision record links to the versions it read and wrote (`state_model_version`, `plan_version`).

- **State-model version history.** Section 4's determinant profile is snapshotted on every update (a filter step, a re-anchor, a cold-start hand-off), each snapshot carrying the determinant point estimates, trends, and confidences at that moment, and linking to the qualifying observations that moved it. A `re_anchor` record points to the snapshot it produced; a decision that *read* the state points to the snapshot it read. This is what lets "am I on track?" be answered as of any past date, and what lets a re-anchoring be explained by the efforts that caused it.
- **Plan version history.** Each plan Section 5 generates, and each material in-flight edit Section 6 applies, is a version; a `re_periodization` record links the old and new plan versions, and every session instance carries the plan version it belongs to. This makes "why is this block different from the last?" answerable by diffing versions.
- **Per-session feature provenance.** Section 3 already attaches to each session's load feature "which metric drove it, the threshold-hour references used, whether the divergence fallback fired, and the quality flags of its inputs" (§3.4). Section 9 requires this provenance be preserved and linked from any decision record whose `inputs` cite that session, so the chain reaches all the way back to the raw-stream quality verdict Section 2 stamped.

Together these three histories plus the decision log are the four persistent records that make the whole loop (§9.2.1) traceable. None is a new computation; each is the durable retention of what a prior section already produced, linked by version identifier.

### 9.3.5 Append-only, immutable, and replayable

Two properties make the log trustworthy as an account rather than a mutable narrative:

- **Append-only and immutable.** A decision, once emitted, is never edited or deleted; a later decision that reverses an earlier one is a *new* record that links to the one it supersedes. The log is therefore a faithful history of what the system did and when, not a snapshot of its current opinion — the distinction that lets an athlete see that Tuesday's downgrade was later found unnecessary, rather than seeing only the final state.
- **Replayable / reproducible.** Because the engine is fully deterministic (§6.1) and every record carries its inputs and the versions it read, any decision can be **re-executed from its record and shown to reproduce the same outcome**. This is the strongest possible form of explainability: not "here is a plausible reason," but "here are the exact inputs and the exact rule, and running them again yields exactly this." It is also the system's verification hook — a regression that changed the engine's behavior would show up as a replay that no longer reproduces the logged outcome.

*Default under uncertainty (retention granularity) — ratified 2026-08-31.* The schema retains every decision record for the life of the athlete's program and, by default, indefinitely thereafter for the athlete's own history; the per-sample raw streams behind the feature provenance are the largest store and are the one item with a tunable retention default (ship: retain raw streams for the current program plus a rolling window, retain derived features and all decision/version records indefinitely). This is an engineering default, not a scientific-uncertainty one, and it does not affect any decision the engine makes; it is ratified and back-ported to the `research/00` Part 3 register (2026-08-31) as a spec-introduced engineering default, and the raw-stream window remains tunable.

## 9.4 Explaining any change on demand

The explanation surface (Section 8 §8.3) answers "why did the system do X?" by reading the four already-computed sources; Section 9 owns the **contract that those sources deliver a complete and faithful answer**. Three guarantees make on-demand explanation possible for *any* change, not just the ones anticipated:

- **Completeness.** Because Section 6 emits a record for every applied change and every safety verdict (§6.1), and because a record is ill-formed without its inputs and rung (§9.3.2), there is no change the athlete can ask about that lacks a record to answer from. The set of answerable "why" questions is exactly the set of decisions the engine made — which is every decision, by construction.
- **Faithfulness at the data layer.** The record presents the numbers the engine already computed and links each to its source (§9.3.1). Section 8's faithfulness rule — the LLM "presents the numbers the engine already computed and never invents new ones" (§8.3.2) — is only enforceable because the data layer guarantees those numbers are on the record and addressable. Section 9 is the reason the language model *can* be faithful: it has the real figures to translate, so a fabricated one is never necessary.
- **Honest "no answer."** When the athlete asks something the log and model do not answer — a counterfactual ("what if I'd run the hard session?"), or a question about a determinant that is low-confidence — the contract is to **return what is on record plus the honest gap**, not a fabricated figure. The schema's `confidence_context` and the state model's confidence labels (§9.5) are what let the surface say "that estimate is provisional and here is what would sharpen it" (Section 8 §8.6) rather than inventing precision. This is the raw-over-derived posture (`research/00` §1.5) applied to explanation: an explanation number, like a decision number, traces to a transparent formula on record or it is not asserted.

The explanation surface is Section 8's; Section 9's deliverable here is the **queryable, complete, provenance-linked, confidence-bearing record set** that makes faithful explanation a property of the system rather than a hope about the language model.

## 9.5 Surfacing confidence: shown, not hidden

Load-bearing finding 8 requires that every determinant carries a confidence, that low confidence widens the guardrails, and that it is **surfaced to the athlete rather than presented as false precision** (`research/00` Part 2 finding 8, §1.6; Section 4 §4.3.2; Section 6 §6.7). Section 4 computes the confidence (as a prediction interval and a low/medium/high label, §4.3.2); Section 6 acts on it (damping up-regulation, §6.7); Section 8 renders it in language (§8.6). Section 9's role is the connective one that makes those possible: **confidence travels with every quantity, all the way to the surface, and is never dropped in transit.**

The schema enforces this in two places. First, every `inputs` tuple and the `confidence_context` field carry the confidence of the determinants a decision leaned on (§9.3.1), so a decision-log query returns not just what the system did but **how sure it was** — a downgrade made on a low-confidence estimate is on the record as such. Second, every state-model snapshot (§9.3.4) carries each determinant's confidence, so an "am I on track?" answer built on a provisional critical-speed estimate can surface that provisionality with the number, exactly as Section 8 §8.6 requires ("your projected race pace is around X, but I'm not yet confident in that … treat it as provisional"). A point estimate is never stored or surfaced without its confidence attached; the schema has no field for a bare number that has lost its interval.

This also closes the transparency loop finding 7 opens (`research/00` Part 2 finding 7; Section 8 §8.6): because the log is queryable by input source (§9.3.3), the athlete can see that the report or constraint they supplied is on the record as an input to a change — the reporting process is engineered so the athlete *sees their input change the plan*, which is what keeps subjective input first-class and keeps the athlete supplying it (`research/03` §1). Confidence surfacing and input-traceability are the two halves of the same commitment: the system shows the athlete both **how sure it is** and **what moved it**, rather than presenting a confident, unattributed plan.

## 9.6 Surfacing the vendor sidecar-versus-own divergence

Section 2 §2.3.6 quarantines every vendor-derived number — Garmin's VO₂max estimate, Training Status, Training Readiness, Body Battery, Performance Condition, the HRV Status classification (Balanced/Unbalanced/Low; `research/00` PRIN-20), Race Predictor, and the rest — in a labeled quarantined sidecar that never feeds a decision — no setting or default lets the coaching logic read it (`research/00` §1.5) — and names two sanctioned uses, neither of which feeds a decision: optional **corroboration** (flagging when the system's own computed metric diverges materially from the vendor's) and showing the athlete a **familiar number**. Section 2 explicitly assigns the *surfacing* of any sidecar-versus-own divergence to Section 9 (§2.3.6, §2.8); this subsection discharges it.

- **The corroboration check as a first-class, logged item.** For each metric where the system computes its own value and a vendor sidecar counterpart exists — most importantly the system's own VO₂max/vVO₂max and durability trend versus Garmin's VO₂max and Race Predictor — Section 9 defines a **corroboration-check record**: `{metric, own_value, own_confidence, vendor_value, divergence, materiality_flag, checked_at}`. The `divergence` is a **presentation-layer comparison** of two numbers the prior sections already produced — the system's own estimate (Section 3/Section 4) and the ingested sidecar value (Section 2 §2.3.6) — not a metric or state computation (the one trivial derivation §9.1 carves out); it is refreshed on the same schedule the state model updates, linked to the state-model version it corroborates, and queryable like any other record. It is a **transparency artifact, not a decision input**: no engine path reads it, exactly as §2.3.6 and raw-over-derived (`research/00` §1.5) require.
- **How it is surfaced.** When the athlete asks (or when a divergence crosses the materiality threshold), the explanation surface (Section 8) presents both numbers side by side, clearly labeled — "your watch estimates VO₂max ≈ 54; our own estimate from your critical-speed data is ≈ 51, and here's why we trust ours" — turning a potentially confusing discrepancy into an explanation of the system's raw-over-derived stance rather than letting the athlete silently trust the more familiar vendor number. The vendor value always carries its §2.3.6 "vendor-derived, not a decision input" marker into the surface, so it is never mistaken for something the plan acted on.
- **Never an escalation.** A material divergence is surfaced for the athlete's awareness and, at most, as a prompt that the system's own estimate is worth sharpening with a qualifying effort (§9.5) — it **never** auto-adjusts the state, never fires a flag, and never enters the arbitration ladder. A divergence means the two estimators disagree, which is expected (the vendor's is an undocumented black box, `research/00` §1.5), not that the system is wrong; the response is transparency, not action.

*Default under uncertainty (materiality threshold) — ratified 2026-08-31.* Section 2 §2.3.6 speaks of a metric diverging "materially" but fixes no number. Section 9 ships a default surfacing threshold — a divergence in the own-versus-vendor value beyond roughly **one own-estimate confidence interval, or ~10% where no interval is defined** — above which a divergence is proactively surfaced rather than shown only on request. This is a spec-introduced default under uncertainty, transparent and tunable, and it governs only *surfacing*, never any decision; it is ratified and back-ported to the `research/00` Part 3 register (2026-08-31), with the confidence-interval basis chosen because a divergence is "material" precisely when it exceeds the uncertainty the system already admits in its own estimate.

## 9.7 What this section hands to the rest of the system, and the loop it closes

Section 9 delivers the loop-closure and explainability layer: the specification of the closed loop as one traceable circuit with the traceability invariant that every prescription is reconstructible back to its raw data and rules (§9.2); the first-class, queryable, append-only decision-log schema that formalizes the records Section 6 emits, with the two mandatory fields the explanation surface depends on, the query axes the athlete's questions demand, the version chain that makes the trace reconstructible, and the replay property that makes explanation reproducible rather than merely plausible (§9.3); the data contract that lets the Section 8 surface explain any change faithfully and admit honestly when it cannot (§9.4); the rule that confidence travels with every quantity to the surface and is shown, not hidden (§9.5); and the surfacing of the Section 2 vendor sidecar-versus-own corroboration checks as transparency, never as a decision (§9.6).

Its relationships to the neighboring sections are all of a piece with its role as closure. **Section 6** is its producer: Section 9 defines the schema, Section 6 emits the entries, and the schema *enforces* Section 6's §6.1 guarantee by rejecting any record that lacks its inputs or its rung. **Section 8** is its principal consumer: Section 9 supplies the complete, queryable, confidence-bearing record set, and Section 8 renders it into language — the boundary Section 8 §8.3 and §8.7 already declared. **Section 4** and **Section 3** are read into the record with their confidences and provenance intact, never recomputed. **Section 2** hands Section 9 the sidecar-divergence surfacing it deferred (§2.3.6, §2.8). **Section 5** and **Section 7** contribute the plan and taper versions the log links to. No section is modified by Section 9; each is made *auditable* by it.

With this section the closed loop the outline describes is complete end to end: inputs (Section 1) → raw data (Section 2) → derived metrics (Section 3) → state estimate (Section 4) → plan (Section 5) → adaptation (Section 6) → recovery and taper (Section 7) → conversational surface (Section 8) → and the trace, log, and explanation that tie them together and make the autonomous system accountable for every change it makes (Section 9). The system pursues one objective — the athlete's expected average race-day pace (`research/00` §1.1) — through a deterministic, raw-data-derived engine whose every decision is protected by the safety constraint above it, bounded by the guardrails around it, made on estimates whose uncertainty it discloses, and recorded so that the athlete, or an auditor, or the engine itself on replay, can always answer the question the whole design exists to answer honestly: *why*.
