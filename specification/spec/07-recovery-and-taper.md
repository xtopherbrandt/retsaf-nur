# Section 7 — Recovery and Taper

*Build-ready specification, Phase 2. This section defines how the system manages **planned recovery** — the down weeks embedded in the training blocks and the pre-race taper — so that accumulated fatigue is shed on schedule and the athlete arrives at the start line optimally recovered and peaked. It is the controller for the two load-reduction structures the plan generator (Section 5) placed but deliberately left as slots: the recovery-week cadence in the mesocycle rhythm (§5.5.2) and the taper as the terminal mesocycle block (§5.5.1). Section 5 fixed *where* those slots sit and *what they are for*; Section 7 owns their **depth, shape, and individualization**, and it owns the mandatory recovery-week guardrails jointly with Section 6. Its terminal purpose is the same as the whole system's — the supreme objective of expected average race pace (`research/00` PRIN-01) — reached here through the best-quantified lever in the field: a taper drives the Training-Stress-Balance (TSB) the fitness/fatigue chart produces (Section 3 §3.5) from strongly fatigued into the modestly-fresh race-day form band, worth a meaningful fraction of race-day pace on its own. It conforms to `research/00` as the decision authority — the supreme objective (`research/00` PRIN-01), the taper decision-register row (`research/00` REG-08), the down-regulate-freely / up-regulate-cautiously asymmetry that makes every reduction here fully autonomous (`research/00` PRIN-13), and the individualization governing rule that decides when the default taper is personalized to the athlete (`research/00` IND-01) — and cites `research/04` §7 for the taper science (including the parasympathetic rebound a well-executed taper produces), `research/05` §2.3–§2.4 for the fitness/fatigue/form and HRV-readiness dynamics the taper drives, and `research/01` §6.3 for the recovery time-courses the down weeks consolidate. Per the project conventions, every rule is stated here in full; the evidence behind it lives in the cited docs and is not re-argued. Every numeric constant below is a **heuristic default** (`research/00` DOC-06), tunable per athlete only under the individualization rule (`research/00` IND-01) and never where it is a design invariant.*

## 7.1 What this section owns, and the boundaries it respects

Section 7 owns the **management of planned recovery**: the depth and shape of the recovery/down weeks embedded in the mesocycle rhythm, the pre-race taper's individualized length (within the distance-scaled default envelope Section 5 sets, §5.5.4), volume-cut depth, decay shape, and the volume-versus-intensity asymmetry, the definition of the race-day target form band, the individualization of the taper by chronic load and race distance, and the verification of the peak against the projected form trajectory and the readiness signals. It is the discharge of the project's build requirement to "manage recovery and taper so the athlete arrives at race day optimally recovered and peaked." Four boundaries fix what this section is and is not.

- **Section 5 places the slots; Section 7 shapes them.** The periodization structure (§5.5) already fixed the taper as the terminal block (default ~2 weeks) and slotted a recovery week into the mesocycle rhythm (default every 4th week); it explicitly deferred their depth, shape, and individualization here (§5.5.1, §5.5.2, and Section 5's own boundary statement in §5.1). Section 7 does not re-place these blocks and does not re-periodize — it fills in the controllers for slots the generator already positioned. When the taper or a recovery week needs to *move* (a changed race date, a compressed calendar, an injury layoff), that is a re-periodization, which is Section 5's generation invoked by Section 6's trigger (§6.3.4), not a Section 7 action.
- **Section 6 triggers and enforces; Section 7 controls the depth.** Section 6's engine reaches the taper block on the calendar and schedules the recovery weeks on their cadence (§6.2.2, §6.10), and it **enforces** the mandatory recovery-week guardrail — the long-term loop's ambition cannot delete a scheduled recovery week (§6.2.2). Those enforcement guardrails are **shared** with this section: Section 6 guarantees the recovery week happens; Section 7 defines how deep it cuts and how it is verified. Every load reduction Section 7 prescribes still passes through Section 6's arbitration ladder (§6.5) and daily readiness gate (§6.2.4) and sits beneath the safety override — a taper does not suspend the injury-risk verdict or the hard flags (§6.4); it operates as the plan frame within which those faster, higher-priority loops still act.
- **Section 7 drives the chart; Section 3 computes it.** The CTL/ATL/TSB chart and its 42/7 EWMA dynamics are Section 3's (§3.5), read here as the quantity the taper steers. Section 7 does not re-implement the chart; it reads the current and projected TSB, sets a target form band, and reduces load so the trajectory lands in that band on race day. The HRV trend and subjective-wellness signals that corroborate the peak are likewise Section 3's (§3.7) and Section 6's readiness fusion (§6.2.4); Section 7 reads them as confirmation, not as new metrics it computes.
- **Section 7 owns plan reductions, which are fully autonomous.** Because every action in this section *reduces* load, it falls entirely on the free side of the down-regulate-freely / up-regulate-cautiously asymmetry (`research/00` PRIN-13; §6.7): a taper and a down week are exactly the "reduce load freely" direction the constitution permits without confidence gating or athlete confirmation. Section 7's reductions therefore apply autonomously (apply-and-notify, `research/00` AUT-01; §6.8), and the only athlete-owned matters remain the two the constitution names — the goal contract (the race date that anchors the taper's timing) and the athlete's clinical action and return-to-run clearance on the safety pathway, which the system itself issues (`research/00` AUT-02, AUT-08). The one place caution re-enters is the *upper* edge of the form band (§7.4.3): over-tapering erodes fitness, so the reduction is bounded below, not because reducing load is risky but because reducing it *too far* stops serving the objective.

Everything in this section operates on the same relative-anchored workout representation Section 5 defined (§5.2) and the same raw-derived chart and readiness metrics Section 3 computes; it introduces no new data structure, only a controller over the existing ones.

## 7.2 Recovery weeks (the down weeks inside the blocks)

A **recovery week** (down week) is a planned, periodic reduction in training load inside a training block whose purpose is to let the accumulated fatigue of the preceding loading weeks dissipate, consolidate the adaptations those weeks stimulated, and return the athlete to a state from which the next loading block can progress (`research/04` §1; `research/01` §6.3). It is distinct from the taper: a recovery week is a *mid-block* reset that the athlete trains through and out of, whereas the taper is the *terminal* peaking structure aimed at a single race day. Section 5 placed the slot (§5.5.2); Section 7 defines its depth and shape and shares its guardrails with Section 6.

### 7.2.1 Cadence and the mandatory guardrail

The default cadence is the one Section 5 shipped — a recovery week every **4th week** (within the every-3-to-4-week range of `research/05` §5.2), tunable by training age and observed recovery (§5.5.2). This cadence is **mandatory, not advisory**: Section 6 §6.2.2 enforces it, and the long-term loop's ambition cannot delete a scheduled recovery week. Section 7 relies on that enforcement and does not re-implement it; what Section 7 adds is the rule for **advancing** a recovery week when the athlete's state calls for one early — a run of amber/red readiness days, a rising monotony/strain flag, a stalled or reversing response trend, or an elevated injury-risk verdict (Section 3 §3.9, Section 6 §6.4) — which pulls the next down week forward rather than waiting for the fixed cadence. Advancing a recovery week is a load reduction and therefore fully autonomous (§7.1); deleting or indefinitely deferring one is not permitted, because that is the direction the guardrail exists to block.

### 7.2.2 Depth and shape

The down week reduces load enough to shed acute fatigue (drop ATL, let TSB rebound toward or above zero) without eroding the chronic base (CTL) the block is building. The shipped default is a **~20–40% reduction in weekly volume** relative to the preceding loading week, achieved primarily by cutting easy-running volume and reducing the volume (not the presence) of the week's quality sessions, while **holding intensity and session frequency roughly constant** so the week consolidates rather than de-trains — the same volume-first, intensity-preserving logic the taper uses (§7.3), applied at lower magnitude and without the taper's progressive decay. Concretely: keep the week's hard-session *touches* at intensity but shorten them, cut the long run and the easy volume, and keep the number of running days so rhythm and neuromuscular sharpness are retained. A recovery week is shallower than a taper because the athlete must train *through* it into the next loading block, not peak from it.

**This 20–40% recovery-week depth is a spec-introduced default under uncertainty.** `research/01` §6.3 establishes the session-type recovery time-courses that motivate periodic down weeks, and `research/05` §5.2 fixes the every-3-to-4-week cadence, but no captured research doc states the depth of a mid-block recovery week. The default is transparent and tunable, it degrades safely (a down week slightly too shallow leaves a little residual fatigue the next down week clears; slightly too deep costs a few days of consolidation, not fitness), and it is **logged as a new open item** for research back-fill and register back-port (§7.10, and `spec_development_plan.md`). It should not be confused with the taper's ~50% cut, which *is* research-grounded (§7.3).

### 7.2.3 Verifying a recovery week worked

The recovery week's effect is read off the same signals the taper is (§7.5), at lower stakes: TSB should rebound over the week (ATL falls faster than CTL), and the readiness signals — the HRV 7-day mean and the subjective wellness z-scores (Section 3 §3.7, §6.2.4) — should recover toward baseline. If they do **not** rebound by the end of the down week, that is evidence the loading was too aggressive, the down week too shallow, or a non-training stressor is present, and Section 6's short-term loop (§6.2.2) responds by extending the reduction or lowering the next block's ramp — the recovery week's failure to restore readiness is itself an input to the slow loops, not a result Section 7 discards.

## 7.3 The taper: the shipped default

The **taper** is the pre-race reduction in training load that lets accumulated fatigue dissipate faster than fitness, so form peaks on race day. It is among the best-quantified interventions in endurance science, and the register adopts its direction, magnitude, and asymmetry directly (`research/00` REG-08; `research/04` §7). The shipped default taper has four levers, each stated with the evidence behind it:

- **Volume is cut substantially — the primary lever.** Total training volume is reduced by a default **~50%** (within the meta-analytic ~40–60% window), because performance is optimized by a large drop in volume and volume is the lever that carries the taper's effect (`research/04` §7, Bosquet meta-analysis). This is the dominant action; the other three levers shape *how* the volume comes down.
- **Intensity is maintained.** High-intensity and race-pace stimuli are **retained** through the taper, at reduced volume — the athlete keeps doing short threshold and goal-race-pace *touches* to stay sharp, because cutting intensity de-trains and blunts the peak (`research/04` §7). In the §5.2 representation this means the taper keeps the `work` segments at their anchored intensity and shrinks their `quantity` and `repeat`, rather than dropping the quality sessions or slowing their targets. Relative anchoring (§5.4.3) keeps every retained pace current even as the state model settles.
- **Frequency is mostly held.** Session frequency is kept roughly constant; large cuts in how *often* the athlete runs are unnecessary and can feel de-conditioning to a trained athlete, so most sessions are kept but shortened (`research/04` §7). The volume cut comes from shorter sessions, not from many rest days.
- **The reduction is progressive (exponential), not a step.** Volume comes down **progressively across the taper on an exponential decay**, which outperforms a single step-down (`research/04` §7). Operationally the weekly (or session-level) volume decays toward race day so the athlete sheds fatigue steadily while never sitting fully idle before the start.

The default **duration is ~2 weeks** (the meta-analytic optimum centers on roughly 8–14 days), individualized by chronic load and race distance per §7.6 (`research/04` §7). The expected payoff is a performance improvement on the order of **~2–3%** — large relative to the pace margins that decide the success metric, and the reason the taper is treated as a first-class controller rather than a formality (`research/04` §7). Unlike several other defaults in this specification, the taper's four levers are **well-established science**, not engineering defaults under uncertainty; the one genuinely open parameter the taper personalizes is its exact duration and depth for the individual, which is precisely what the adaptive system tunes (§7.6).

## 7.4 The taper as a TSB controller

Operationally, the taper is a **Training-Stress-Balance controller**: it reduces daily load so that ATL (the 7-day fatigue proxy) falls quickly while CTL (the 42-day fitness proxy) erodes only slightly, carrying TSB (= CTL − ATL, Section 3 §3.5) from the strongly negative values of hard loading up into a modestly-positive **race-day form band** precisely on race day (`research/04` §4.2, §7; `research/05` §2.3). This is the read-out that turns the taper from a fixed template into a controlled trajectory the system can verify and adjust.

### 7.4.1 The race-day target form band

The default target is to land TSB in a **modestly-positive band of roughly +5 to +25** on race day, athlete- and event-dependent (`research/04` §4.2, the practitioner form-band heuristic; §7). This band is the taper's set-point, and it is bounded on **both** sides deliberately:

- **The lower bound (~+5)** guards against under-tapering: a TSB still near zero or negative on race day means fatigue was not sufficiently shed and the athlete races tired, leaving fitness masked by residual fatigue.
- **The upper bound (~+25)** guards against over-tapering: a TSB driven too high means either the taper was too deep/long or was extended past the point of shedding fatigue into eroding fitness (CTL bleeding away) and losing race sharpness ("staleness"). More positive is not monotonically better, which is why the band is a target interval, not a floor.

The band is a **practitioner heuristic default**, flagged as such per the uncertainty-explicit convention: the individual optimum within (or slightly outside) it varies by athlete and event, and the system refines the athlete's own best-form TSB from their response history where enough race and hard-tune-up data exist to identify it (§7.6; `research/00` REG-27, IND-01). The band's width is also why the taper reads the projected trajectory rather than aiming at a single number.

### 7.4.2 Steering the trajectory

Given the current chart state (CTL, ATL, TSB, Section 3 §3.5) at taper onset and the days remaining to `race_date`, the controller projects the TSB trajectory forward under the planned exponential volume decay and checks where it lands. Because the EWMA dynamics are fully specified (§3.5.2), the projection is deterministic: the controller can compute, for a given decay shape and depth, the race-day TSB, and it shapes the decay so the projection lands in the band. If the projection falls **short of the band** (TSB still too low on race day), the controller deepens the volume cut or, within the timing constraints §7.6 sets, lengthens the taper; if it **overshoots** (TSB above the band before race day, with CTL eroding), the controller shallows the cut or shortens the front of the taper so the athlete keeps more fitness. Intensity is never traded away to steer TSB — reducing intensity would de-train and is off the table (§7.3); the controller moves only volume and duration.

### 7.4.3 The over-taper bound is the one place caution re-enters

Section 7's reductions are otherwise on the free side of the asymmetry (§7.1), but the upper edge of the form band is where reducing *more* stops helping and starts hurting: past the fitness-preserving point, additional load reduction erodes CTL and dulls race sharpness. The controller therefore treats the upper bound as a real constraint, not a soft preference — it will not keep cutting volume merely because cutting is the "safe" direction. This is the sense in which the taper is bounded below in load: enough reduction to shed fatigue and reach the band, and no more. It is the one asymmetry-exception this section carries, and it is a *fitness-preservation* bound, not a safety one — the safety override (§6.4) and daily readiness gate (§6.2.4) continue to operate above it throughout the taper.

## 7.5 Verifying the peak

A projected TSB in the band is a *model* prediction; the system corroborates it against the athlete's actual physiological state before trusting that the peak has landed, because the chart is load-only and blind to sleep, illness, and life stress (Section 3 §3.5.4). The corroborating signals are the readiness signals Section 3 and Section 6 already compute (§3.7, §6.2.4), read here in the specific pattern a good taper produces:

- **Parasympathetic (HRV) rebound.** A well-executed taper is typically accompanied by the athlete's 7-day rolling ln rMSSD **rebounding toward or above baseline** as fatigue clears (`research/04` §7; `research/05` §2.4; the HRV trend Section 3 §3.7 emits). An HRV rebound in step with the rising TSB is positive confirmation the taper is working.
- **Restored subjective wellness.** The five-item wellness z-scores (fatigue, sleep, soreness, stress, mood; §6.2.4) returning toward or above baseline is the subjective corroboration, and — consistent with the first-class status of self-report (`research/00` ARCH-07; `research/03` §1) — it is weighted equally with the objective rebound.

The verification rule is conjunctive in the reassuring direction and disjunctive in the cautioning one, following the conservative conflict-resolution the constitution fixes (`research/00` PRIN-05; §6.6): TSB climbing into the band **and** HRV/wellness rebounding is a confirmed peak. But if TSB has climbed into the band while **HRV fails to rebound or wellness stays suppressed**, the system does **not** declare the peak on the chart alone — a divergence this specific (fresh by the load model, still fatigued or unwell by the autonomic and subjective signals) is exactly the case the conservative rule was written for: it points to a non-training stressor (illness incubating, life stress, sleep debt) the load-only chart cannot see. The taper controller responds by holding or gently extending the reduction and routing the discrepancy into Section 6's readiness fusion and injury-risk review (§6.2.4, §6.4), rather than adding load to "sharpen" against a signal it cannot trust. Because self-report also skews toward **under-reporting near a goal race** (athletes suppress symptoms they fear will cost them the race; `research/03` §6, and Section 6 §6.6), missing or implausibly flat taper-week reports are themselves treated as a caution, not as reassurance.

## 7.6 Individualization by chronic load and race distance

The default taper (§7.3) is the starting template; the system personalizes its **duration and depth** to the athlete and the event, which is the one part of the taper the science leaves to the adaptive layer (`research/04` §7; `research/00` REG-08: "then individualized by chronic load and distance"). Three inputs drive the individualization, and it is governed by the individualization rule (`research/00` IND-01) — the taper is personalized only where the athlete's own data identifies a better setting than the default, otherwise the default holds with honest confidence.

- **Chronic load (CTL).** A higher chronic training load carries more accumulated fatigue to shed, so a higher-CTL athlete warrants a somewhat **longer and/or deeper** taper to bring ATL down far enough for TSB to reach the band, while a lower-CTL athlete reaches the band with a shorter, shallower taper (`research/04` §7). The controller reads CTL at taper onset (Section 3 §3.5) and scales the taper length within the ~8–14-day window (extending modestly beyond it only for the highest chronic loads and longest events) and the volume-cut depth within the ~40–60% window accordingly.
- **Race distance.** Longer, more glycogen-demanding races warrant a somewhat **longer** taper; shorter, faster events taper shorter (`research/04` §7). The default lands the marathon toward the long end of the window (~2–3 weeks) and the 5K/10K toward the short end (~7–10 days), consistent with the distance parameterization Section 5 already applies to the whole plan (§5.5.4, which sets the distance-scaled *default* taper length) — Section 7 then individualizes the taper's exact length, depth, and shape within that Section-5-set default envelope (the ownership Section 5 §5.1 cedes here), adjusting length only within the ~8–14-day window; any change large enough to *move* the block is a Section 5 re-periodization on Section 6's trigger (§6.3.4), not a Section 7 action (§7.1).
- **Response history.** Where the athlete has enough prior races and hard tune-ups for the system to have observed the TSB at which they have historically performed best, that observed best-form TSB displaces the generic +5-to-+25 default as the target, and the observed taper length that produced it informs the duration — the individualization rule's "identifiable from the athlete's own data and backed by enough quality data to beat the default" test applied to the taper (`research/00` IND-01). Until that history exists, the population default band and the distance/CTL-scaled duration hold, at honest confidence, and the taper is verified by the trajectory-plus-readiness check of §7.5 rather than by a personalized set-point.

The individualization is adaptive, not one-shot: the taper controller re-projects the TSB trajectory as each taper-week's actual load lands and re-shapes the remaining decay to keep the projection in the band (§7.4.2), so a taper that is shedding fatigue faster or slower than modeled is corrected in flight rather than run open-loop from a fixed template.

## 7.7 How the taper is represented, placed, and adapted in flight

The taper introduces no new data structure. It is expressed entirely in Section 5's workout representation (§5.2) and reached through Section 6's engine (§6.10), which keeps it inside the same closed loop as the rest of the plan.

- **Representation.** A taper week is an ordinary microcycle of §5.2 workout objects with reduced `quantity`/`repeat` on the easy-volume and quality segments, the quality segments' `intensity` anchors **unchanged** (intensity maintained, §7.3), the session count held (frequency held), and the whole tagged `phase: taper` (§5.2.5). Because paces are relative-anchored (§5.4.3), any state-model settling during the taper re-anchors the retained race-pace and threshold touches automatically, with no rewrite — the athlete's sharpening work stays calibrated to their current fitness right up to the start line.
- **Placement.** Section 5 placed the taper as the terminal block on the calendar (§5.5.1); Section 6 reaches it on the calendar and hands its depth/shape to this section (§6.10). Section 7 does not decide *when* the taper starts — that is a function of `race_date` and the Section-5-set length envelope — it decides *how* the volume comes down within that block. If the race date changes, the taper is re-placed by a Section 5 re-periodization on Section 6's trigger (§6.3.4), not by Section 7.
- **Adaptation in flight, under the arbitration ladder.** The taper is still governed by Section 6's arbitration every day it runs (§6.5). The daily readiness gate (§6.2.4) may still down-regulate a taper session — a red day in taper week swaps a sharpening touch for rest exactly as in any other week — and the safety override (§6.4) still sits above everything: a hard flag during the taper stops the plan and surfaces the safety pathway regardless of how close the race is. The taper controller's own adjustments (deepen/shallow the decay to steer TSB, §7.4.2) are load reductions and reshaping *within* the taper block, composed with the faster loops by the same ladder — the readiness gate can soften a taper day the controller planned, never harden it (the down-regulate-freely / up-regulate-cautiously asymmetry, `research/00` PRIN-13; §6.7). Every taper adjustment is applied autonomously and logged with the signal that drove it (apply-and-notify, `research/00` AUT-01; §6.8), so Section 9 can explain on demand why a given taper session was shaped as it was.

## 7.8 Heat/altitude acclimation-block timing (the deferred open item)

Section 6 §6.9 resolved that acclimatization is **not** a tracked daily determinant but a plan-scope preparation concern, and homed the detailed prescription of a heat/altitude acclimation block jointly in Section 5 (generation) and Section 7 (taper-adjacent timing), leaving it as a logged open item for a later addendum. Section 7 records the **taper-timing constraint** the addendum must respect, and otherwise leaves the block unspecified, because Section 1's conservative no-acclimatization default remains in force and nothing here blocks on it:

- An acclimation exposure block for a hot or high-altitude goal race is placed in the **final weeks of the run-up**, which overlaps the peak/taper window this section controls, so its timing cannot be set independently of the taper. The constraint the addendum must honor is that **acclimation adaptations decay** — heat acclimation in particular fades within days-to-weeks of removal — so the block must be timed close enough to race day that its adaptation is still present on race day, yet integrated with the taper so the acclimation stimulus does not reintroduce the very fatigue the taper exists to shed. Reconciling "close to the race" (for the adaptation) with "shedding fatigue into the race" (the taper's job) is the core timing problem the addendum owns.
- Until such a block is actually prescribed and executed, Section 1 §1.4.4 continues to default to **no acclimatization** in the pace target (`research/00` IND-01, the individualization rule; Section 6 §6.9), so the taper controller plans against the conservative projection and credits no acclimation adaptation it cannot verify.

This is carried as a **jointly-homed open item** (Section 5 / Section 7) for the addendum, not specified here; it does not block this section (§7.10, and `spec_development_plan.md`).

## 7.9 Post-race recovery (scope boundary)

The system's supreme objective terminates at the goal race — expected average pace *over the goal race* (`research/00` PRIN-01) — so the specification's closed loop runs up to and including race day, and formal **post-race recovery** (the reverse taper, the return to base training after the race) is **out of scope** for this build. Two things are worth stating so the boundary is deliberate rather than an omission: first, the criteria-based graded return-to-run pathway Section 6 §6.4.4 defines already covers the case where post-race soreness or a race-incurred injury requires managed reintroduction of running, so the safety-relevant part of "after the race" is specified; second, a full post-race recovery-and-rebuild protocol (its own light reverse-periodization toward the next macrocycle) is a natural **future addendum** but is not required by the definition of done for the current objective. Where the athlete sets a *new* goal race after the current one, that is a fresh program start with a new goal contract (§5.8 step 1), not a Section 7 concern.

## 7.10 What this section hands to the rest of the system

Section 7 delivers the recovery and taper controller: the depth and shape of the mid-block recovery weeks and the rule for advancing them, with their mandatory cadence enforced by Section 6 and shared here (§7.2); the shipped default taper — ~2 weeks, ~50% volume cut, exponential decay, intensity maintained, frequency mostly held, worth ~2–3% (§7.3); the taper as a TSB controller that steers the chart Section 3 produces into a race-day form band of roughly +5 to +25, bounded on both sides against under- and over-tapering (§7.4); the verification of the peak against the projected TSB trajectory plus the HRV/parasympathetic and subjective-wellness rebound, with the conservative rule for when they diverge (§7.5); the individualization of taper duration and depth by chronic load, race distance, and response history under the individualization governing rule (§7.6); the taper's expression in Section 5's existing workout representation and its in-flight adaptation under Section 6's arbitration ladder and safety override (§7.7); and the taper-timing constraint the deferred acclimation-block addendum must honor (§7.8).

Three explicit hand-offs carry forward. **Section 6** shares the mandatory recovery-week guardrails this section shapes and continues to govern every taper and down-week day through its arbitration ladder, daily readiness gate, and safety override — Section 7 sets the frame (how deep the reduction goes and what form band it targets), Section 6 decides what the athlete does each day within it, and the two meet at the shared guarantee that a scheduled recovery week is never deleted and a taper never suspends safety. **Section 3** produces the TSB trajectory and the HRV/wellness readiness signals this section's taper drives toward the race-day form band and reads back to verify the peak; Section 7 adds no metric, only a controller over Section 3's chart. **Section 9** receives the decision-log entries every recovery-week and taper adjustment emits — the signal that drove each reduction and the rule that composed it — and formalizes them into the queryable explanation surface, so the athlete can ask why a taper session was shaped as it was and get the numbers the engine actually used. The division of labor with the neighboring sections is preserved throughout: Section 5 generates the periodized plan and places the taper and recovery slots, Section 6 runs the plan day by day and protects the athlete, Section 3 computes the fitness/fatigue/form and readiness metrics, and Section 7 shapes the planned reductions — the down weeks and the taper — so that the athlete arrives at the goal race with fatigue shed, fitness preserved, and form peaked in service of the one metric the whole system optimizes: expected average race pace.
