# Section 8 — Conversational Coach Interface and Autonomy Boundary

*Build-ready specification, Phase 2. This section defines the **Conversational Coach Interface**: the natural-language surface through which the athlete probes the system's decisions and supplies life constraints, injury/soreness reports, and plan-change requests in free text. It is an **input/output layer on top of** the deterministic closed loop Sections 1–7 define — never a second adaptation path and never a gating mechanism. The deterministic five-timescale engine (Section 6) remains the single source of truth: the language model translates in two directions only — athlete free-text into the structured inputs the engine already consumes, and the engine's computed state and decision log into plain-language explanation — while the engine decides. This section conforms to `research/00` as the decision authority: the fully-autonomous apply-and-notify posture and the plan-versus-goal ownership boundary (`research/00` §1.8–§1.9, which resolve the autonomy question `research/05` §7 raised; `decisions/01` conforms to them and records its own earlier checkpoint framing as superseded, `research/00` DEC-01), raw-over-derived (`research/00` §1.5), and explainability as a first-class constraint (`research/00` §1.6). It formalizes the design record `decisions/01` (Conversational Coach Interface), and honors the routing Section 6 §6.8.1 already specifies rather than re-inventing it. The subjective and injury instruments the parser targets are `research/03`'s (the five-item wellness questionnaire, session-RPE, the pain traffic-light with localized-pain mapping); Section 8 puts a language front-end on them, it does not redefine them. Per the project conventions, every rule is stated here in full; the evidence behind it lives in the cited docs and is not re-argued.*

---

## 8.1 What this section owns, and the boundaries it respects

Section 8 owns the **conversational I/O layer**: the natural-language parser that turns athlete free-text into the structured fields the engine's entry points consume, the explanation surface that turns the decision log and the current model state into plain-language answers, and the athlete-availability/constraints model that the parser populates for the weekly loop to read. It is the discharge of the `decisions/01` decision to include a conversational coach as a product differentiator that "dissolves the opacity of the underlying formulae and tables" while keeping the transparent, raw-data-derived engine underneath as the actual differentiator. Four boundaries fix what this section is and is not.

- **Section 8 translates; Section 6 decides.** The language model proposes and explains; the deterministic engine decides (`decisions/01`; `research/00` §1.8). Every input the parser produces is fed to an entry point Section 6 already defines, and every plan change it can trigger routes through the same arbitration ladder (§6.5) as every other signal. The LLM never touches the derived-metric formulas (Section 3) or the state-model math (Section 4); those stay deterministic and owned, which is what keeps chat from becoming "a second, opaque adaptation path" (`decisions/01`; `research/00` §1.5). Section 8 is the only place in the system where a language model participates in the control loop at all, and its participation is bounded to translation in both directions.
- **Section 8 is not a gate.** Chat is **not a confirmation or acknowledgment checkpoint** (`research/00` §1.8; `decisions/01`, "Relationship to the autonomy posture"). Plan changes never wait on the athlete to read, acknowledge, or approve them. The interface lets the athlete *probe* an already-applied change to understand why it happened — explanation on demand, not an approval step — and lets the athlete *inform* the system of outside scheduling constraints and negotiate schedule changes, which route into the weekly loop as inputs. An "acknowledged-notification" step for major changes was considered and deliberately rejected (`research/00` §1.8; `decisions/01`).
- **Section 8 surfaces the engine's numbers; it invents none.** Explanation reads the decision log Section 6 emits (§6.1) plus the state model (Section 4), the derived metrics (Section 3), and the gap-to-goal decomposition (Section 1 §1.4 / Section 4 §4.7), and presents numbers the engine *already computed*, never new ones the LLM derives (`decisions/01`, read/explain direction). This is the raw-over-derived and explainability posture applied to the presentation layer: the same determinism that makes every change explainable (Section 6 §6.1) is what lets the explanation be faithful rather than a plausible-sounding fabrication.
- **Section 8 owns no adaptation authority; the athlete owns only the goal contract and, on the safety pathway, their own clinical action and clearance.** The interface can supply inputs and request changes, but cannot override guardrails or write directly to metrics or state (`decisions/01`, guardrails). The only two matters that sit outside the engine's autonomous authority are the **goal contract** and, on the **safety pathway**, the athlete's own clinical action and return-to-run clearance (`research/00` AUT-02, GOAL-02; Section 6 §6.8) — and both are matters the system does not own, not checkpoints on what it does. The safety override itself is the engine's: it applies the override automatically and deterministically and issues the instruction the athlete must act on (`research/00` AUT-08). Section 8 is where those two hand-offs to the athlete are *surfaced and negotiated*, but it does not expand them: everything the system legitimately owns (the plan) still applies without a confirmation step.

Everything the interface reads or writes operates only on the raw-derived metrics, the owned state model, and the deterministic decision log — never on the quarantined vendor sidecar (§2.3.6; `research/00` §1.5), except to show the athlete a familiar vendor number when they ask for one, clearly labeled as corroboration and never as a driver of a decision.

## 8.2 The two directions, and why the layer is thin

`decisions/01` fixes the guiding principle: the chat interface is **a new I/O layer, not a new decision layer**. It sits on top of the existing closed loop and does not replace or duplicate any part of it. The language model's role is translation in both directions, and nothing more:

- **Read / explain (transparency).** Turn the engine's computed state and decisions into plain-language explanation — answering "why is today easy?" by reading the decision-log record plus the current model state, and presenting the numbers the engine already computed (§8.3).
- **Write / negotiate (constraints and reports).** Turn athlete free-text into the structured inputs the engine already consumes, routed to existing entry points, not a new one — life constraints to the weekly loop, injury/soreness/subjective reports to the `research/03` instruments (§8.4).

The layer is deliberately **thin**: its entire job is to move information across the natural-language boundary in a form the deterministic engine can consume or the athlete can understand. Keeping it thin is what preserves the project's raw-over-derived, transparent-formula philosophy (`research/00` §1.5–§1.6) and what keeps the freedom-to-operate posture safe — the differentiator is the transparent engine underneath, and the interface must be describable as *explaining and translating*, never as *generating, personalizing, or deciding* the plan (`research/06` §11; `decisions/01`, open follow-up). The two directions are specified in §8.3 and §8.4; the boundary that bounds both is §8.5.

## 8.3 Read / explain: the decision log as plain-language explanation

The explanation direction rests on a guarantee Section 6 already makes: **every applied adaptation is logged with its inputs and the rule that fired** (`research/00` §1.6; Section 6 §6.1; `research/05` §5.6). `decisions/01` promotes that log "from an implied byproduct to a first-class, queryable explanation record"; the record's schema and its promotion to a first-class queryable structure are **Section 9's** to formalize (Section 6 §6.1, §6.10). Section 8 is the *consumer* of that record: the natural-language surface that reads it and renders it into an answer.

### 8.3.1 What an explanation reads

When the athlete asks why something happened — "why is today easy?", "why did my long run move to Sunday?", "why did you cut this week's volume?", "am I on track for my goal?" — the interface assembles the answer from four already-computed sources, and only these:

1. **The decision-log record(s)** for the change in question — the inputs that were present, and the arbitration rung that fired (Section 6 §6.5, §6.1). This is the spine of every "why" answer: the ladder is deterministic, so the reason a day resolved as it did is a fact on record, not a reconstruction.
2. **The current state model** — the determinant estimates, their trends, and their confidences (Section 4 §4.2). This answers "where do I stand?" and grounds "am I on track?" against the gap-to-goal.
3. **The derived metrics** — the load chart (CTL/ATL/TSB, Section 3 §3.5), the HRV trend and readiness verdict (Section 3 §3.7; Section 6 §6.2.4), the recent-session response features (Section 3 §3.6). This answers "why did the system read me as fatigued / recovered / improving?"
4. **The gap-to-goal decomposition** — the determinant-decomposed distance between the current race-pace projection and the goal contract (Section 1 §1.4; the decomposition Section 4's profile enables, §4.7). This answers "why is the plan emphasizing threshold right now?" by naming the largest addressable gap the long-term loop is targeting (Section 6 §6.2.1).

### 8.3.2 The faithfulness rule

The explanation **presents the numbers the engine already computed and never invents new ones** (`decisions/01`, read/explain direction). The language model may summarize, paraphrase, and translate a formula's output into lay terms — "your heart-rate recovery relative to pace has been drifting, which reads as accumulated fatigue" — but it may not compute a fresh metric, re-derive a determinant, or state a number the deterministic pipeline did not produce. If the athlete asks a question the log and model do not answer, the correct behavior is to say so and, where relevant, name what the system would need to answer it (for example, that a determinant is low-confidence and the system is waiting on qualifying maximal efforts to sharpen it, §8.6), not to fabricate a plausible figure. This rule is the presentation-layer expression of raw-over-derived (`research/00` §1.5): a number the athlete sees in an explanation traces to a transparent formula on record, exactly as every number that drives a decision does.

## 8.4 Write / negotiate: free-text into structured engine inputs

The write direction parses athlete free-text into the **same structured fields the forms would produce** and feeds them to the engine entry points that already consume them (`decisions/01`, write/negotiate direction; Section 6 §6.8.1). The parser adds no new entry point and no new decision path; it is a front-end to the inputs the engine already reads. It recognizes the **four input types `decisions/01` fixes** — life/scheduling constraint, injury report, subjective/wellness report, and plan-change request — each routed to an existing consumer, plus an **explanation request** that routes to §8.3. (The plan-change request is a meta-request: the parser resolves it into one of the other kinds — a scheduling constraint or a goal-contract proposal — rather than writing a plan change directly, per §8.4.1.) The natural-language ↔ structured-input contract is the enumerable core of this section and is stated in full below.

### 8.4.1 The recognized input types and their routing

| Input type | Free-text example | Structured fields produced | Routes to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Life / scheduling constraint** | "I'm travelling Thu–Sun next week and can only run 40 min on weekdays." | Unavailable days; per-day time budget; travel window; (optional) equipment/terrain limits | The **athlete-availability / constraints model**, read by the **short-term weekly-microcycle loop** (Section 6 §6.2.2; `research/05` §5.2) |
| **Injury / soreness report** | "Left Achilles is a 3/10, tight in the morning but eases once I'm warm; been creeping up over two weeks." | Pain location (→ implied tissue type); 0–10 intensity; overnight-settle (yes/no); week-over-week trend | The **injury-risk verdict** (Section 6 §6.4) via the **pain traffic-light with localized-pain mapping** (`research/03` §3.5) |
| **Subjective / wellness report** | "Slept badly all week, legs feel dead, pretty stressed with work." | Five-item wellness (fatigue, sleep, soreness, stress, mood); sleep duration/quality; lightweight illness flag | The **readiness fusion** (Section 6 §6.2.4) via the **five-item wellness questionnaire** and illness check (`research/03` §3.3–§3.4) |
| **Plan-change request** | "Can we move my hard session to Wednesday?" / "I want to add a tune-up 10K in three weeks." | The change requested, resolved to either a scheduling constraint (→ weekly loop) or a goal-contract proposal (→ athlete-owned input, §8.5) | The **short-term loop** (§6.2.2) for schedule changes; the **goal-contract hand-off** (§8.5.2) for goal changes |
| **Explanation request** | "Why is today easy?" / "Am I on track?" | The target of the question (a specific change, the current state, or the goal projection) | The **explanation surface** (§8.3) |

Two properties of this table are load-bearing. First, the parser produces **exactly the fields the corresponding form produces** — for a pain report, the four fields `research/03` §3.5's traffic-light consumes (location, intensity, overnight-settle, weekly trend); for a wellness report, the five items `research/03` §3.3 defines plus the sleep and illness fields §6.2.4 fuses — so a free-text report and a form submission are indistinguishable downstream. Second, a **plan-change request is not itself a plan change**: the parser resolves it to one of the existing input kinds. A schedule negotiation becomes a constraint the weekly loop re-solves around; a goal change becomes an athlete-owned goal-contract input (§8.5.2). The interface never writes a plan change directly.

### 8.4.2 The athlete-availability / constraints model

`decisions/01` requires "a new, explicit athlete-availability / constraints model" for the weekly loop to consume. Section 8 owns this model as the structured target of parsed scheduling constraints. It carries, per upcoming week: which days are available for training; the per-day time budget; declared travel windows; and any standing recurring constraints the athlete has stated (for example, "never a hard session on Mondays"). The **short-term weekly-microcycle loop reads this model and re-solves the week's placement within its existing ramp-rate and monotony guardrails** (Section 6 §6.2.2), then reports back what moved — the negotiation loop `decisions/01` specifies and Section 6 §6.2.2 already implements as an input-and-report cycle. The constraints model is an *input to* the weekly planner, never an override of it: a constraint can reshape *where* load is placed, but it cannot lift the ramp-rate cap, delete a mandatory recovery week, or force a hard session onto an amber/red day (§8.5.1). When the athlete's constraints make the planned week infeasible within the guardrails, the loop reduces or reshapes load within them and reports the trade-off, exactly as it would for any other constraint.

### 8.4.3 Reports feed the instruments, not the decision

A parsed injury or wellness report is fed to the deterministic instruments (`research/03`) that Section 6 already fuses; the report is an *input to* the verdict, not the verdict itself. The pain report populates the traffic-light Section 6 §6.4.2 evaluates — conditioned on the tissue type the location implies, so a reported Achilles pain is read against the tendon rule and a reported focal shin pain against the bone-stress exception — and the wellness report populates the readiness fusion Section 6 §6.2.4 z-scores against the athlete's own rolling baseline. Because those instruments are trend-based (`research/00` §1.4; Section 6 §6.6), a single free-text report is weak evidence on its own and is interpreted against the athlete's history exactly as a single form submission would be. The interface's job is faithful capture — turning "a 3/10 that eases once I'm warm and has been creeping up" into the precise fields — not interpretation; the interpretation is the deterministic engine's.

## 8.5 The autonomy boundary

The hard boundary is the heart of this section, and it is fixed by the decision authority (`research/00` §1.8–§1.9), not chosen here. It has three parts: safety hard-flags fire deterministically regardless of framing (§8.5.1); the only two athlete-owned matters are the goal contract and the athlete's clinical action and return-to-run clearance on the safety pathway (§8.5.2); and everything else is apply-and-notify, so chat is never a gate (§8.5.3).

### 8.5.1 Safety hard-flags fire deterministically, regardless of framing

The **safety override and the injury hard-flags fire deterministically, regardless of how the conversation is framed** (`decisions/01`, guardrails; `research/00` §1.2 rung 1, §1.8; Section 6 §6.4.3). The language model **cannot talk the system past a red flag** — bone-stress-pattern pain, night pain, focal bony tenderness, systemic illness beyond a mild cold, RED-S indicators, acute/traumatic pain or swelling — and the **athlete cannot talk the system into unsafe loading** or past a mandatory recovery week (Section 6 §6.2.2, §6.4.3; `research/03` §5.4, §6). This is enforced structurally, not by the LLM's judgment: chat-originated inputs enter through the same parser-to-instrument path as form inputs (§8.4), and the hard flags are evaluated by the deterministic verdict (Section 6 §6.4) that sits at the top of the arbitration ladder (Section 6 §6.5.2, rung 1). A persuasively-worded request to "just let me do the hard session, I feel fine" is parsed into a subjective report like any other and fused against the objective signals under the conservative-reading-wins rule (Section 6 §6.6); it cannot bypass the verdict, because the verdict is not in the LLM's hands. The escalation pathways `research/03` §6 defines remain authoritative, and the safety instruction the system surfaces is athlete-facing because the athlete must act on it (§8.5.2).

### 8.5.2 The only athlete-owned matters: the goal contract and the clinical action on the safety pathway

Consistent with the plan-versus-goal ownership boundary (`research/00` §1.9; Section 6 §6.8), the **only two matters the athlete owns are the goal contract and, on the safety pathway, their own clinical action and return-to-run clearance** (`research/00` AUT-02) — and both are matters the system does not own, not checkpoints on the plan it does own. The safety override and the pathway's instruction are the system's (`research/00` AUT-08).

- **The goal contract** — the three athlete-owned fields: the target pace (`goal_pace_target`), the race date (`race_date`) and the race distance (`distance_m`) — is the athlete's (`research/00` §1.9). The system may **propose** a goal change through the interface — for instance when the race-pace projection has drifted far from the target, or when an injury layoff has made the goal infeasible (Section 6 §6.4.4) — but never applies one unilaterally. When the system initiates such a proposal, it follows the fixed order of remedy (`research/00` §1.9): the interface leads with a **revised goal pace** the current projection can support on the existing race date (the primary, least-disruptive remedy), and offers a **later race date** only as the costlier secondary alternative — never a change of race distance. That ordering governs only what the system *proposes*; the athlete may still request any goal-contract change of their own accord. When the parser recognizes a plan-change request as a *goal* change — a pace change ("let's target 3:30 instead of 3:20"), a date change ("move the race to the fall"), or otherwise — it routes to the goal-contract hand-off: the change takes effect **only as an athlete-supplied input**, exactly as the goal is set at program start, and then triggers a re-periodization through Section 6 §6.3.4 / Section 5. This is the one write where the athlete's confirmation is intrinsic — not because chat is gating the plan, but because the goal is not the system's to set.
- **The safety pathway** — a stop-and-escalate, return-to-run, or seek-clinical-assessment instruction the system issues itself (Section 6 §6.4.3–§6.4.4; `research/00` AUT-08) — is surfaced to the athlete because the athlete must act on it: the clinical action and the return-to-run clearance it calls for are the athlete's (`research/00` AUT-02). The interface presents it plainly and does not soften or negotiate it; the LLM has no authority to downgrade a safety instruction, and a conversational request to ignore it does not (§8.5.1). The system **diagnoses nothing** — it detects patterns and routes to care (`research/03` §6) — and the interface's language must reflect that: it reports what the system observed and what action it recommends, without asserting a diagnosis.

Everything else — the plan, and the safety override itself (`research/00` AUT-08) — sits inside the system's autonomous authority (`research/00` GOAL-01; Section 6 §6.8).

### 8.5.3 Everything else is apply-and-notify; chat routes through the same arbitration

For every matter the system owns, the posture is **apply-and-notify** (`research/00` §1.8; Section 6 §6.8): the daily gate's softening, the recent-workout tweaks, the weekly re-solve, and full re-periodizations all take effect and are *then* surfaced with their explanation. Chat is where that notification and its explanation live, but it is **not the mechanism by which the change takes effect** — the change already applied. **Every chat-originated plan change routes through the same arbitration ladder (Section 6 §6.5) as every other signal** (`decisions/01`, guardrails; Section 6 §6.8.1): a scheduling constraint the athlete supplies is re-solved by the weekly loop within its guardrails, a soreness report is fused into the readiness gate, and neither can produce an outcome the ladder would not otherwise permit. This is what makes the interface safe to expose: it can only push inputs into a deterministic machine whose guardrails it cannot move, and it can only read back what that machine computed.

## 8.6 Surfacing confidence: low confidence shown, not hidden

Load-bearing finding 8 requires that every determinant carries a confidence, that low confidence widens the guardrails, and that it is **surfaced to the athlete rather than presented as false precision** (`research/00` Part 2 finding 8, §1.6; Section 4 §4.6). The conversational interface is the surface where that requirement is discharged athlete-facing. When an explanation touches a determinant or a projection, the interface **shows the confidence, not just the point estimate**: an "am I on track?" answer built on a low-confidence critical-speed estimate says so — "your projected race pace is around X, but I'm not yet confident in that; it's based on limited recent hard efforts, so treat it as provisional" — rather than presenting a crisp number that overstates what the system knows. This is the same low-confidence state Section 4 §4.6 already widens the guardrails on and Section 6 §6.7 already treats as weaker evidence for up-regulation; Section 8's job is to make that uncertainty legible to the athlete instead of hiding it behind a confident sentence.

Surfacing confidence also closes the transparency loop `research/00` Part 2 finding 7 opens: the reporting process is engineered so the athlete *sees their input change the plan* (`research/00` Part 2 finding 7; `research/03` §1). When a report or constraint the athlete supplied moves the plan, the interface reports back what moved and why — closing the loop that keeps subjective input first-class and keeps the athlete supplying it. Where a determinant is low-confidence and the system is waiting on the qualifying maximal efforts (Section 5 §5.2.6) that would sharpen it, the interface can say so, turning the confidence label into an actionable explanation ("a race or hard time-trial in the next couple of weeks would let me firm this up") rather than an apology.

## 8.7 What this section hands to the rest of the system

Section 8 delivers the conversational I/O layer: the natural-language parser and its structured-input contract (§8.4), the explanation surface that reads the decision log and current model state (§8.3), the athlete-availability/constraints model the weekly loop consumes (§8.4.2), the autonomy boundary that keeps chat an input/explanation layer rather than a gate (§8.5), and the confidence-surfacing behavior that makes Section 4's uncertainty legible to the athlete (§8.6).

Its hand-offs are all to entry points the rest of the system already defines, which is the point of a thin layer. **Section 6** receives every parsed input at the entry points §6.8.1 names — scheduling constraints into the short-term weekly-microcycle loop (§6.2.2), injury/soreness/subjective reports into the injury-risk verdict (§6.4) and the readiness fusion (§6.2.4) — and every chat-originated change routes through the §6.5 arbitration ladder, with the §6.4.3 hard flags firing deterministically regardless of framing. **Section 5** is reached only indirectly: a goal-contract change the athlete enacts (§8.5.2) triggers the Section 6 §6.3.4 re-periodization that calls the Section 5 generator; the interface never calls the generator itself. **Section 4** and **Section 3** are read, never written: the explanation surface presents their determinant estimates, confidences, and derived metrics (§8.3), and the confidence-surfacing behavior (§8.6) renders Section 4 §4.6's low-confidence labels athlete-facing. **Section 9** is the interface's principal upstream dependency for explanation: it formalizes the decision-log records Section 6 emits (§6.1) into the first-class, queryable structure the explanation surface reads, and it owns surfacing the Section 2 sidecar-divergence corroboration checks to the athlete; Section 8 is the language front-end on top of that queryable record. The division of labor holds throughout: the deterministic engine decides and logs, and the conversational layer translates — free-text into the engine's inputs, and the engine's log and state into the athlete's language — without ever becoming a second place where the plan is decided.
