|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-07 | **Which E003 block ships first** | §3.3 GAP substrate (the spec's own dependency order) vs. §3.7 HRV trend vs. athlete profile + §3.4 load | **§3.7 HRV trend** | The only block with no blockers. §3.4–§3.6 need threshold pace, HR_rest and HR_max, which come from Section 4 (E004, unbuilt) or an athlete profile that does not exist — config carries four fields and a grep across both packages finds no anchor of any kind ([[IDEA-035]]). | 2026-09-07 | **Which column the trend reads** — §3.7.1 says `rmssd_precomputed`; F004 ships `resting_rmssd_ms` as "the field E003 reads" | Amend §3.7 vs. keep §3.7 as authority and branch by tier vs. defer | **Amend §3.7** to `resting_rmssd_ms`, reading `hrv_source_tier` separately for baseline discipline | Section 3 predates F004's T065 and was never reconciled: the authority document and the shipped feature disagreed at exactly the seam this feature builds on. The resolved column is the better design and was deliberate — the consumer never asks which tier produced a value, which is what makes the `ln()` call safe. Branching by tier would re-implement F004 and reintroduce the reasoning the column exists to remove. |
| 2026-09-07 | **The band statistic** — §3.7.3 says `0.5·CV(ln rMSSD)`, but the worked example was plainly computed another way | `0.5·SD(ln)` vs. `0.5·CV(raw)` applied multiplicatively vs. keep §3.7.3 literal and fix only the example | **`0.5 · SD(ln rMSSD)`, amending §3.7.3** | Raised by the adversarial critic and verified numerically. `CV = SD/mean` is a ratio to the origin, and a log scale's origin is arbitrary: re-expressing the same readings in seconds rather than milliseconds flips the sign of `CV(ln)` and inverts the band (`band.lo > band.hi`), while `SD(ln)` is unchanged. `0.5·CV(ln)` is also ≈4× too narrow (0.0138 vs 0.0526 on a realistic series). `SD(ln)` is numerically indistinguishable from the literature's `0.5·CV` of the **raw** series (0.0526 vs 0.0525), so the register's intent survives and only the statistic expressing it is corrected. **The defect is in the authority document**, so amending §3.7.1/§3.7.2 alone would have shipped a corrected input into an uncorrected formula. |
| 2026-09-07 | **The band floor** | 0.05 vs. **0.01** | **0.01** | At 0.05 the floor sits *above* an ordinary athlete's `0.5·SD(ln)` ≈ 0.05, so it would be the band for everyone and the computed half-width would never be exercised — a discriminator with no reachable negative case. At 0.01 it fires only for a genuinely degenerate series and both branches are testable. |
| 2026-09-07 | **Baseline window placement** relative to the 7-day window | Overlapping (baseline ends at D) vs. **disjoint** (baseline ends at D-7) | **Disjoint: baseline `[D-66, D-7]`, window `[D-6, D]`** | An overlapping baseline puts the judged week inside its own reference, so a sustained suppression drags its band down and self-clears — against §3.7.4's rule that a single good morning does not clear an accumulated suppression. Also states all windows as closed local-date intervals, removing an off-by-one two builders could resolve differently while both passing. |
| 2026-09-07 | **What re-establishes the baseline** — §3.7.3 resets on tier change, but excluding F004's pre-amendment window makes readings vanish with *no* tier change | Generalise to any coverage gap vs. special-case the window vs. do nothing | **Reset on sustained tier change, coverage gap > 21 local days, or a timezone change** | The amendment window is not a tier change, so §3.7.3 as written would let one baseline silently span two eras — the anti-mixing hazard the rule exists to prevent. A gap rule catches it and covers device loss, layoff and season breaks with one mechanism rather than encoding a historical migration permanently. 21 days survives a taper or a holiday; the asymmetry favours keeping a hard-won baseline. Timezone change was added after the critic noted the whole history re-buckets, which can merge or split local days and silently move a verdict. |
| 2026-09-07 | **How the gap is measured** | Stored rows vs. **the post-exclusion series** | **The post-exclusion series** | Pre-amendment rows are stored rows that are not readings. Measuring on rows would mean the gap never opens across the amendment window and the reset this rule was invented for would never fire. |
| 2026-09-07 | **Baseline window and "adequately established"** — §3.7.3 names neither | 30d/10 vs. **60d/14** vs. 90d/20 | **60 local days, minimum 14 readings** | 60 days is the lineage the register cites and is long enough for a stable dispersion without spanning a training block. 14 keeps a suppression verdict reachable for an athlete capturing a few times a week while giving SD enough spread to mean something. |
| 2026-09-07 | **Thin and degenerate data** | Floor + window minimum vs. window minimum only vs. neither | **Both guards** | Without a floor a metronomic athlete gets a razor-thin band and is punished for consistency. Without a window minimum a single reading becomes a "trend", contradicting §3.7.4. Both thresholds are echoed in the response so a consumer can see which fired. A baseline of fewer than 2 readings asserts **no band at all** — SD is undefined at N=1 and the response must not carry a band object built from nothing. |
| 2026-09-07 | **What defines a day** — `start_time` is UTC; "morning", "7-day" and "21-day gap" are local | Configured timezone vs. bucket by UTC vs. derive per session from FIT | **Configured `athlete_timezone`** | A 06:00 capture at UTC+13 lands on the previous UTC day, silently shifting which readings fall in the window. Deriving per session from FIT is better in principle but F003 does not persist the offset, so it needs an ingestion change and a fallback regardless. |
| 2026-09-07 | **Whether `athlete_timezone` may have a default** | Default `"UTC"` vs. **required, no default** | **Required, following F001** | The first draft asserted a safe `"UTC"` default in one sentence. `AppConfig`'s docstring carries the ratified rule verbatim — "No field has a default: an incomplete config is a validation error rather than a silently-absorbed default" — and F004 rejected a default on the same grounds. The substance agrees: an unconfigured athlete at UTC+13 gets wrong buckets with no error, which is exactly a silently-absorbed default producing wrong output. Costs a migration note like F004's. `tzdata` becomes a runtime dependency (Windows stdlib `zoneinfo` has no system tzdb, so `Pacific/Auckland` would otherwise raise), and the zone is validated at config load rather than at request time, where it would surface as a 500. |
| 2026-09-07 | **Multiple captures in one day** | Highest tier then earliest vs. average vs. earliest regardless of tier | **Within the baseline tier, earliest** | Averaging blends two physiologically different moments of the waking autonomic curve, and the spec's smoothing belongs at the 7-day level. Earliest-regardless-of-tier risks feeding a Health Snapshot into a chest-strap baseline — the mixing §3.7.3 forbids. Refined after the critic showed the original "highest tier then earliest" rule interacts badly with tier filtering: applied before the tier filter it drops any day whose best capture is off-tier, even when a usable on-tier reading exists. Order is now normative: exclude, resolve tier, filter, then collapse. |
| 2026-09-07 | **Fallback when no tier sustains a baseline** | Highest tier present vs. **tier with the most readings** | **Tier with the most readings** | The original rule let a single borrowed chest-strap capture demote an athlete's established 45-reading Health Snapshot baseline to `n=1` and blank the verdict for weeks. §3.7.3 anticipates that athlete explicitly and rules the other way: the occasional higher-tier capture is corroboration, never merged into the band. |
| 2026-09-07 | **`rr_valid_fraction` re-gating** | Re-apply the <80% gate here vs. **treat it as discharged upstream** | **Discharged upstream** | The critic showed the scenario was unreachable: `hrv_classification.py` states that a failed gate leaves `hrv_source_tier` and `rmssd_precomputed` `None`, so such a capture has no tier, is not in this feature's series, and is not in F004's window predicate either. The original spec's canonical `excluded[]` example was the one entry the endpoint could never emit. Surfacing gated captures for explainability would require reading `activity_tag` and `quality_flags`, which is a scope increase; recorded here as a possible later addition rather than smuggled in. |
